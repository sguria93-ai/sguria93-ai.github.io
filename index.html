Calculus • Intermediate

Definite Integration by Parts

Evaluate the following definite integral using integration by parts: $$\int_{1}^{e} x \ln(x) \, dx$$
Quick Check (Value of the integral):
Step 1: Choose Parts via LIATE Rule
Let $u = \ln(x)$ (Logarithmic) and $dv = x \, dx$ (Algebraic).
Differentiating $u$: $$du = \frac{1}{x} \, dx$$ Integrating $dv$: $$v = \frac{x^2}{2}$$
Step 2: Apply the Integration by Parts Formula
$$\int u \, dv = uv - \int v \, du$$ $$\int_{1}^{e} x \ln(x) \, dx = \left[ \frac{x^2}{2} \ln(x) \right]_{1}^{e} - \int_{1}^{e} \frac{x^2}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{x} \, dx$$ $$= \left( \frac{e^2}{2} \ln(e) - \frac{1^2}{2} \ln(1) \right) - \frac{1}{2} \int_{1}^{e} x \, dx$$
Step 3: Evaluate Remaining Integral & Limits
Since $\ln(e) = 1$ and $\ln(1) = 0$: $$= \frac{e^2}{2} - \frac{1}{2} \left[ \frac{x^2}{2} \right]_{1}^{e} = \frac{e^2}{2} - \frac{1}{4}(e^2 - 1) = \frac{2e^2 - e^2 + 1}{4} = \frac{e^2 + 1}{4}$$
Final Answer: $$\frac{e^2 + 1}{4} \approx 2.09726$$
Linear Algebra • Intermediate

Eigenvalues of a $2 \times 2$ Real Matrix

Find the two real eigenvalues $\lambda_1, \lambda_2$ (with $\lambda_1 \le \lambda_2$) of the matrix: $$A = \begin{pmatrix} 4 & 2 \\ 1 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$$
Quick Check (Enter comma-separated values λ1, λ2):
Step 1: Set up the Characteristic Equation
The eigenvalues satisfy $\det(A - \lambda I) = 0$: $$\det \begin{pmatrix} 4 - \lambda & 2 \\ 1 & 3 - \lambda \end{pmatrix} = 0$$
Step 2: Expand the Determinant Polynomial
$$(4 - \lambda)(3 - \lambda) - (2 \times 1) = 0$$ $$\lambda^2 - 7\lambda + 12 - 2 = 0 \implies \lambda^2 - 7\lambda + 10 = 0$$
Step 3: Factor the Characteristic Polynomial
$$(\lambda - 2)(\lambda - 5) = 0 \implies \lambda_1 = 2, \quad \lambda_2 = 5$$ Notice that $\text{Tr}(A) = 4 + 3 = 7 = \lambda_1 + \lambda_2$ and $\det(A) = 12 - 2 = 10 = \lambda_1 \lambda_2$.
Final Answer: $$\lambda_1 = 2, \quad \lambda_2 = 5$$
Algebra & Optimization • Beginner

Global Extreme Value of a Quadratic Function

Find the global maximum value $M$ of the parabolic objective function: $$f(x) = -2x^2 + 12x - 7$$
Quick Check (Maximum value of f(x)):
Method 1: First Derivative Test (Calculus)
Compute the critical point by setting $f'(x) = 0$: $$f'(x) = -4x + 12 = 0 \implies 4x = 12 \implies x^* = 3$$ Since $f''(x) = -4 < 0$, $x^* = 3$ is a strict global maximum.
Method 2: Completing the Square (Algebraic)
$$f(x) = -2(x^2 - 6x) - 7 = -2\left((x - 3)^2 - 9\right) - 7$$ $$= -2(x - 3)^2 + 18 - 7 = -2(x - 3)^2 + 11$$ Because $(x - 3)^2 \ge 0$, the maximum occurs when $(x - 3) = 0$.
Final Answer: $$f_{\max} = f(3) = 11$$
Probability • Intermediate

Bayesian Diagnostic Testing

A rare medical condition has a prevalence of $0.5\%$ in a population ($P(D) = 0.005$). A diagnostic test has a true positive rate (sensitivity) of $98\%$ and a false positive rate of $2\%$. If a randomly selected patient tests positive, calculate the posterior probability $P(D \mid +)$ that the patient actually has the disease.
Quick Check (Answer as decimal or %):
Step 1: Identify Given Priors & Conditionals
$$P(D) = 0.005, \quad P(D^c) = 1 - 0.005 = 0.995$$ $$P(+ \mid D) = 0.98, \quad P(+ \mid D^c) = 0.02$$
Step 2: Total Probability Law for Denominator $P(+)$
$$P(+) = P(+ \mid D)P(D) + P(+ \mid D^c)P(D^c)$$ $$P(+) = (0.98 \times 0.005) + (0.02 \times 0.995) = 0.0049 + 0.0199 = 0.0248$$
Step 3: Apply Bayes' Theorem
$$P(D \mid +) = \frac{P(+ \mid D)P(D)}{P(+)} = \frac{0.0049}{0.0248} = \frac{49}{248} \approx 0.19758$$
Final Answer: $$P(D \mid +) = \frac{49}{248} \approx 19.76\%$$
Calculus • Intermediate

Indeterminate Form Limit Evaluation

Evaluate the following trigonometric limit without a calculator: $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos(3x)}{x^2}$$
Quick Check (Value of the limit):
Method 1: L'Hôpital's Rule
Direct substitution yields $\frac{1 - 1}{0} = \frac{0}{0}$. Differentiating top and bottom: $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{3\sin(3x)}{2x}$$ Since this is still $\frac{0}{0}$, apply L'Hôpital's rule a second time: $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{9\cos(3x)}{2} = \frac{9(1)}{2} = \frac{9}{2}$$
Method 2: Maclaurin Taylor Series Expansion
Recall $\cos(\theta) = 1 - \frac{\theta^2}{2!} + \mathcal{O}(\theta^4)$. Setting $\theta = 3x$: $$\cos(3x) = 1 - \frac{(3x)^2}{2} + \dots = 1 - \frac{9x^2}{2} + \dots$$ $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \left(1 - \frac{9x^2}{2}\right)}{x^2} = \frac{\frac{9x^2}{2}}{x^2} = \frac{9}{2}$$
Final Answer: $$\frac{9}{2} = 4.5$$
Linear Algebra • Advanced

Invertibility and Determinant of a $3 \times 3$ Matrix

Determine the determinant $\det(B)$ of the matrix: $$B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 2 \\ 2 & -1 & 3 \\ 4 & 1 & 8 \end{pmatrix}$$
Quick Check (Value of det(B)):
Step 1: Expand Along the First Row
Notice Row 1 contains a zero entry, making Laplace expansion efficient: $$\det(B) = 1 \cdot \begin{vmatrix} -1 & 3 \\ 1 & 8 \end{vmatrix} - 0 \cdot \begin{vmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 8 \end{vmatrix} + 2 \cdot \begin{vmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 4 & 1 \end{vmatrix}$$
Step 2: Evaluate $2 \times 2$ Minors
$$\begin{vmatrix} -1 & 3 \\ 1 & 8 \end{vmatrix} = (-1)(8) - (3)(1) = -8 - 3 = -11$$ $$\begin{vmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 4 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = (2)(1) - (-1)(4) = 2 + 4 = 6$$
Step 3: Combine Terms
$$\det(B) = 1(-11) + 2(6) = -11 + 12 = 1$$
Final Answer: $$\det(B) = 1 \quad (\text{Matrix is non-singular and invertible})$$
